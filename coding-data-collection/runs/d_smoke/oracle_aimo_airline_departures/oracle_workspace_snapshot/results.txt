AIMO Problem 1: Airline Departure Analysis
=========================================
Problem: Three airline companies operate flights from Dodola island.
The first company departs every 100 days, the second every 120 days,
and the third every 150 days. What is the greatest positive integer d
for which there will be d consecutive days without a flight from Dodola
island, regardless of the departure times of the various airlines?

Let's solve this problem with a mathematical proof and computational analysis:
Step 1: Analyzing A100 and A150 only
-------------------------------------
We first prove that there is a period of 99 days after an A100
departure during which no A150 plane takes off.

As in the proof, consider a period of 301 days which starts with
an A150 departure on Day 0, followed by departures on Day 150 and Day 300.

Case (i): When the first A100 departure (Day x) is between 0 and 50:
  If A100 departs on Day 0, next A150 departure is on Day 150
  This gives a quiet period of 149 days
  ✓ This confirms a quiet period of at least 99 days
  If A100 departs on Day 25, next A150 departure is on Day 150
  This gives a quiet period of 124 days
  ✓ This confirms a quiet period of at least 99 days
  If A100 departs on Day 50, next A150 departure is on Day 150
  This gives a quiet period of 99 days
  ✓ This confirms a quiet period of at least 99 days

Case (ii): When the first A100 departure (Day x) is between 51 and 99:
  If first A100 departs on Day 51, second A100 is on Day 151
  Next A150 departure after second A100 is on Day 300
  This gives a quiet period of 148 days
  ✓ This confirms a quiet period of at least 99 days
  If first A100 departs on Day 75, second A100 is on Day 175
  Next A150 departure after second A100 is on Day 300
  This gives a quiet period of 124 days
  ✓ This confirms a quiet period of at least 99 days
  If first A100 departs on Day 99, second A100 is on Day 199
  Next A150 departure after second A100 is on Day 300
  This gives a quiet period of 100 days
  ✓ This confirms a quiet period of at least 99 days

Conclusion: Regardless of when A100 and A150 start their schedules,
there will always be a period of at least 99 consecutive days without
any departure from either airline.

Step 2: Adding A120 to the analysis
----------------------------------
We prove that there are 79 consecutive days on which no departure
of any airline happens, including the A120 planes.

As in the proof, we define Day 0 to be when an A100 flight departs
and there is no A150 flight before Day 100.

Case 1: When the first A120 departure (Day y) is ≤ 20 or ≥ 80:
  If first A120 departure is on Day 20:
  There are at least 79 days between this and the next departure (A100 on Day 100)
  If first A120 departure is on Day 80:
  There are at least 79 days between A100 departure on Day 0 and A120 on Day 80

Case 2: When the first A120 departure (Day y) is between 20 and 60:
  If first A120 departure is on Day 40:
  There will be an A120 departure on Day 280 (= 280)
  No A120 departure strictly between Day 300 and Day 380
  This gives 79 consecutive quiet days between Day 301 and Day 379

Case 3: When the first A120 departure (Day y) is between 61 and 80:
  If first A120 departure is on Day 70:
  There will be an A120 departure on Day 70+240 (= 310)
  No subsequent departures before Day 400
  This gives 79 consecutive quiet days ending on Day 309

Conclusion: Regardless of when each airline starts its schedule,
there will always be a period of at least 79 consecutive days without any departure.

Step 3: Verifying that 79 is optimal
----------------------------------
We show that this bound can be attained with the following configuration:
- A100 departs on Day 0
- A120 departs on Day 80
- A150 departs on Day 120

The departure days are then:
0, 80, 100, 120, 200&200, 270, 300, 320, 400, 420, 440, 500, 560, 570
(modulo 600, i.e., it repeats every 600 days)

Note: 200&200 indicates that both A100 and A120 depart on day 200.

Longest run of consecutive days without flights: 79 days
This is obtained 3 times in the 600-day cycle:
- From Day 1 to Day 79
- From Day 121 to Day 199
- From Day 321 to Day 399

✓ This confirms that 79 is the optimal answer.

Summary:
1. When considering only A100 and A150, there is always a period of
   at least 99 consecutive days without departures.
2. When A120 is added, this reduces to 79 consecutive days.
3. We verified that 79 is attainable with the specific configuration
   described in the solution.

Therefore, the answer to the AIMO problem is \boxed{79}.
