\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

We model a single migration event: a source site must move its active inference
sessions to other sites before a deadline $D$ so that the source can shed power.
The decision is per session---\emph{where} it lands and \emph{how} its serving
state is rebuilt there. We call the controller that plans and routes this migration
\emph{Queue-Haul} (Fig.~\ref{fig:sites}): it queues sessions at the source and hauls
their state to the destinations.

Sessions are grouped into classes $q\in\mathcal{Q}$ by model $m(q)$ and
token-length bucket; class $q$ holds $n_q$ jobs of context length $T_q$. A job is
rebuilt at a destination $\ell\in\mathcal{L}$ by one of two actions.
\emph{Replay} ($R$) ships the compact context ($\beta_q T_q$ bytes, with
$\beta_q\!\approx\!4$\,B/tok) and recomputes the KV cache through prefill, costing
$T_q/\rho_q$ GPU-seconds on a warm instance. \emph{State transfer} ($S$) ships the
KV cache itself ($\eta_q T_q$ bytes, $\eta_q$ the per-token KV size) and skips
prefill, paying instead to ingest that volume. The decision variables are the
fractional job counts $x^R_{q\ell},x^S_{q\ell}\ge 0$ and the \emph{stranded} count
$z_q\ge 0$ of jobs not rebuilt by the deadline. We relax $x,z$ to the reals, so the
program is a planning model for how much work moves, where, and by which action.
The relaxation models fractional jobs whose loads are integrated over the window
$[0,D]$; it does not model intra-window ordering or transient queueing. Integer
rounding and discrete-event queue simulation are deferred.

\paragraph{Resource model.} Each destination has three resources shared over the
window $[0,D]$: network ingress $\Lambda_\ell$, and, per model, a warm-instance
pool $W_{\ell m}$ that serves both prefill and state ingest (the latter at
per-instance rate $\mu_{\ell m}$). Capacities scale with the deadline,
\[
C^{\mathrm{net}}_\ell=\Lambda_\ell D,\quad
C^{\mathrm{pfill}}_{\ell m}=W_{\ell m} D,\quad
C^{\mathrm{ing}}_{\ell m}=W_{\ell m}\mu_{\ell m} D,
\]
and the induced loads are affine in $x$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
L^{\mathrm{net}}_\ell &= \textstyle\sum_q\big(\beta_q T_q\,x^R_{q\ell}+\eta_q T_q\,x^S_{q\ell}\big),\\
L^{\mathrm{pfill}}_{\ell m} &= \textstyle\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_m}\tfrac{T_q}{\rho_q}\,x^R_{q\ell},
\qquad
L^{\mathrm{ing}}_{\ell m} = \textstyle\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_m}\eta_q T_q\,x^S_{q\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Replay loads prefill but is light on the network for long contexts; state transfer
loads network and ingest but never prefill. Write $p_i(x)=L_i(x)/C_i$ for the
normalized pressure on resource $i$ in the index set $\mathcal{I}$ (one network term
per site, one prefill and one ingest term per active model--site pair). Every stage
shares the same base constraints,
\[
\sum_{\ell}\big(x^R_{q\ell}+x^S_{q\ell}\big)+z_q=n_q\ \ \forall q,
\qquad
p_i(x)\le 1\ \ \forall i\in\mathcal{I}:
\]
conservation (every job moves or is stranded) and a window budget on every resource.

\paragraph{Why staged, not weighted.} The goals---move the most work, spread load,
rebuild quickly, treat classes fairly---have different units, and one weighted
objective would bury whichever matters most. We minimize them
\emph{lexicographically}: each stage optimizes one objective, then freezes its
optimum as a constraint for the next.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Stage 1 (migrate the most; LP).}
\[
\min_{x,z\ge 0}\ Z=\sum_q z_q \quad\text{s.t. base constraints.}
\]
$Z^\star$ is the fewest jobs that must be stranded under the deadline, and
$N-Z^\star$ (with $N=\sum_q n_q$) the most that can migrate. The objective is
swappable: replacing $\sum_q z_q$ with a max--min floor or a proportional-fair
log-utility $\sum_q n_q\log(1-z_q/n_q)$ trades total throughput for per-class
equity and yields a fairness certificate ($\alpha^\star$ or $U^\star$) that later
stages preserve.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Stage 2 (balance the load; LP).} Among all plans that
strand only $Z^\star$ jobs, take the one that leaves the most headroom:
\[
\min_{x,z,\phi}\ \phi \quad\text{s.t. }
p_i(x)\le\phi\ \forall i,\ \ \textstyle\sum_q z_q=Z^\star,\ \text{base constraints.}
\]
$\phi^\star$ is the lowest achievable peak utilization: $\phi^\star<1$ means the
plan leaves slack, $\phi^\star=1$ means some resource is saturated. Stage 2 is the
workhorse. It is where the action mix (replay vs.\ transfer) and the destination
routing are actually decided, and its dual prices (Sec.~\ref{sec:methods}) are the
analytical handle we lean on throughout.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Lexicographic refinements.} Three further stages break
ties without disturbing $Z^\star$ or $\phi^\star$. They are scored by the per-class
rebuild cost
\[
r_q=\tfrac{1}{n_q}\big[\textstyle\sum_\ell(c^R_{q\ell}x^R_{q\ell}+c^S_{q\ell}x^S_{q\ell})+d^{\mathrm{miss}}z_q\big],
\]
where $c^a_{q\ell}$ is the unloaded reconstruction time and $d^{\mathrm{miss}}\!=\!2D$
the stranded-job penalty. The three stages are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stage 2b (QP):} minimize the congestion potential $\tfrac12\sum_i p_i(x)^2$.
\item \textbf{Stage 3 (LP):} minimize the worst-class cost $H=\max_q r_q$.
\item \textbf{Stage 4 (LP):} minimize the spread $V=\max_q|r_q-\bar r|$.
\end{itemize}
Each stage adds the prior optimum as a constraint, so the Stage-1 fairness
certificate survives into the final plan.

\paragraph{Convexity.} Every stage is convex. The feasible set is a polyhedron
(affine conservation and capacity constraints, nonnegativity); the Stage-1, 3, and
4 objectives are linear, Stage 2 is a linear min--max in epigraph form, and the
Stage-2b potential is a nonnegative sum of squares of affine maps. So the pipeline
is LP\,$\to$\,LP\,$\to$\,(QP)\,$\to$\,LP\,$\to$\,LP, every local optimum is global,
and strong duality holds---which is exactly what makes the Stage-2 prices in the
next section meaningful.
