\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We ask three things of the program. Does it choose sensible reconstruction actions?
Does it behave the way a deadline-constrained system should? And do its dual prices
predict sensitivity where the envelope identity applies? The first-order convergence
study lives in Sec.~\ref{sec:methods}; here we report the exact solve.

\paragraph{The action mix is sensible, and its crossover is endogenous.} At the
realistic bandwidths of Table~\ref{tab:setup} the optimizer migrates everyone
($Z^\star=0$, $\phi^\star=0.93$ at $D{=}300$s) and rebuilds $80$--$100\%$ of jobs by
\emph{replay}. The reason is cost: shipping the compact context and recomputing the
KV cache is cheaper than moving tens of KiB/tok of state across the link. State
transfer only becomes attractive when the network is abundant. Figure~\ref{fig:action}
sweeps the bandwidth scale. The replay share falls steeply from ${\sim}92\%$,
crossing $50/50$ at about $16.7\times$ the default bandwidth and yielding to state
transfer beyond it. Halving the prefill rate is a separate sweep, and it slides that
crossover down to $8.4\times$. This is the multi-session analogue of the single-session boundary
$\lambda^\star=\rho(\eta-\beta)$ from Sec.~\ref{sec:formulation}: the same physics,
but now set \emph{inside} the optimization by shared congestion rather than read off
a per-session formula.

\paragraph{The deadline is the control that matters.} Figure~\ref{fig:feasible}
sweeps $D$. Migration feasibility climbs sharply, from $29\%$ of jobs at $D{=}1$s to
$91\%$ at $60$s and $100\%$ by $300$s for $10$k sessions, and doubling the load
pushes the full-migration point out to ${\sim}600$s. Recall that $r_q$ is the
per-class rebuild cost and $H^\star=\max_q r_q$ the worst-class cost. It mirrors the
feasibility transition. While any class is still stranded, the
worst class \emph{is} a stranded one, so $H^\star$ rides the miss penalty
$d^{\mathrm{miss}}=2D$ exactly. The moment full migration becomes feasible, it
collapses to the true worst-class rebuild cost, ${\approx}11$s. A diagnostic LP that
forces full migration at a tight deadline confirms network and prefill saturate
together while ingest stays slack.

\paragraph{The dual-predicted slope matches finite differences in the interior.} The
Stage-2 prices $\pi_i$ name the binding resource and, because every capacity is
linear in $D$, predict how the peak moves with the deadline. Figure~\ref{fig:envelope}
(right) overlays the dual-predicted slope $-\tfrac1D\sum_i\pi_i \hat p_i$ on the
finite-difference slope of $\phi^\star(D)$. The two agree closely while the program
is interior: at $D{=}90$s, $-7.1$ vs $-7.3\!\times\!10^{-3}$; at $D{=}273$s, $-7.8$
vs $-8.0\!\times\!10^{-4}$. They separate once $\phi^\star$ pins at $1$, exactly
where the envelope identity stops applying. There, extra deadline is absorbed by
Stage 1, which strands fewer jobs, rather than by lower pressure, so the realized
slope falls to zero while the prediction does not. The left panel reads off the
scarce model: Qwen3-235B, with the tightest prefill supply, carries the largest
prefill price. One LP solve thus yields both an allocation and a sensitivity model
that holds wherever the program is interior.

\section{Conclusion}

We recast site power-down as a deadline-constrained migration of stateful LLM
sessions and solved it as a short lexicographic stack of convex programs. The exact
solve recovers structure that matches the physics: replay dominates at realistic
bandwidth, with an endogenous crossover to state transfer. Its Stage-2 dual delivers
interpretable congestion prices, whose envelope-theorem prediction matches finite
differences wherever the program is interior. The first-order solvers reproduce the
right qualitative ordering, mirror descent over subgradient, but are not yet
accurate. Three steps follow immediately: tighten the first-order solvers, round the
fractional plan, and validate it against a discrete-event queue.
