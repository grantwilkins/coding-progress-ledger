\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We ask three things of the program: does it choose sensible reconstruction
actions, does it behave the way a deadline-constrained system should, and are its
dual prices trustworthy. The first-order convergence study lives in
Sec.~\ref{sec:methods}; here we report the exact solve.

\paragraph{The action mix is sensible, and its crossover is endogenous.} At the
realistic bandwidths of Table~\ref{tab:setup} the optimizer migrates everyone
($Z^\star=0$, $\phi^\star=0.93$ at $D{=}300$s) and rebuilds $80$--$100\%$ of jobs by
\emph{replay}: shipping the compact context and recomputing the KV cache is simply
cheaper than moving tens of KiB/tok of state across the link. State transfer only
becomes attractive when the network is abundant. Figure~\ref{fig:action} sweeps the
bandwidth scale: the replay share falls from ${\sim}92\%$ to ${\sim}10\%$, crossing
$50/50$ at about $16.7\times$ the default bandwidth, and halving the prefill rate
slides that crossover down to $8.4\times$. This is the multi-session analogue of
the single-session boundary $\lambda^\star=\rho(\eta-\beta)$ from
Sec.~\ref{sec:formulation}: the same physics, but now set \emph{inside} the
optimization by shared congestion rather than read off a per-session formula.

\paragraph{The deadline is the control that matters.} Figure~\ref{fig:feasible}
sweeps $D$. Migration feasibility climbs sharply---from $29\%$ of jobs at $D{=}1$s
to $91\%$ at $60$s and $100\%$ by $300$s for $10$k sessions---and doubling the load
pushes the full-migration point out to ${\sim}600$s. The worst-class cost mirrors
this: while any class is still stranded, the worst class \emph{is} a stranded one,
so $H^\star$ rides the miss penalty $d^{\mathrm{miss}}=2D$ exactly; the moment full
migration becomes feasible it collapses to the true worst-class rebuild cost,
${\approx}11$s. A diagnostic LP that forces full migration at a tight deadline
confirms network and prefill saturate together while ingest stays slack.

\paragraph{The dual prices are trustworthy.} The Stage-2 prices $\pi_i$ name the
binding resource and, because every capacity is linear in $D$, predict how the peak
moves with the deadline. Figure~\ref{fig:envelope} (right) overlays the
dual-predicted slope $-\tfrac1D\sum_i\pi_i r_i$ on the finite-difference slope of
$\phi^\star(D)$: they agree closely while the program is interior (at $D{=}90$s,
$-7.1$ vs $-7.3\!\times\!10^{-3}$; at $D{=}273$s, $-7.8$ vs $-8.0\!\times\!10^{-4}$),
and separate once $\phi^\star$ pins at $1$---there extra deadline is absorbed by
Stage 1 stranding fewer jobs, not by lower pressure, so the realized slope falls to
zero while the prediction does not. The left panel reads off the scarce model:
Qwen3-235B, with the tightest prefill supply, carries the largest prefill price. One
LP solve thus yields both an allocation and a validated sensitivity model.

\section{Conclusion}

We recast site power-down as a deadline-constrained migration of stateful LLM
sessions and solved it as a short lexicographic stack of convex programs. The exact
solve recovers structure that matches the physics---replay dominates at realistic
bandwidth, with an endogenous crossover to state transfer---and its Stage-2 dual
delivers interpretable congestion prices that pass an envelope-theorem sensitivity
check. The first-order solvers reproduce the right qualitative ordering (mirror
descent over subgradient) but are not yet accurate; tightening them, then rounding
the fractional plan and validating it against a discrete-event queue, are the
immediate next steps.
