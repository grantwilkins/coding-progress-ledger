\section{Replay vs.\ KV Transfer: Data and Assumptions}
\label{app:migration-ratio}

This appendix gives the numbers behind \texttt{migration\_ratio.py} and its two
figures. Everything here is arithmetic on published specs. Nothing is measured.

\subsection{What we compute}

A session holds $T$ tokens of context. To move it to another site we can send
the prompt and prefill again (\emph{replay}), or send the KV cache
(\emph{transfer}). We plot the ratio of the two times,
\[
  \frac{t^{R}}{t^{KV}} \;=\; \frac{\Phi(T)/\mathrm{EFF}}{8\,\eta(T)/\lambda},
\]
where $\Phi(T)$ is prefill FLOPs, $\mathrm{EFF}$ is sustained throughput,
$\eta(T)$ is KV bytes, and $\lambda$ is the link rate in Gbps. Above $1$, send
the KV cache. Below $1$, replay. The crossover rate is
$\lambda^\star = 8\,\eta(T)/t^{R}(T)$.

Prefill has two terms:
\[
  \Phi(T) \;=\; \underbrace{2 A T}_{\text{FFN}} \;+\;
  \underbrace{2 H_q (d_{qk}+d_v)\, P(T)}_{\text{attention}},
\]
with $A$ the active parameter count, $H_q$ the query heads, and $P(T)$ the
number of causal (query, key) pairs.

\subsection{Hardware}

One instance is $8\times$ H100 SXM. Dense BF16 peak is $989.5$\,TFLOP/s per GPU.
That is half of the $1{,}979$\,TFLOP/s NVIDIA quotes, because their number
assumes 2:4 sparsity~\cite{nvidia2024h100}. At 35\% model FLOP
utilization~\cite{chowdhery2023palm},
\[
  \mathrm{EFF} = 8 \times 989.5\,\text{TFLOP/s} \times 0.35 \approx 2.77\,\text{PFLOP/s}.
\]
MFU is the only free parameter. The ratio scales linearly in it.

\subsection{Counting attention pairs}

Plain causal attention computes $T^2/2$ pairs per layer. Three architecture
features change that count.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Compression.} CSA and HCA squeeze $m$ tokens into one KV
entry~\cite{deepseekv4}. A layer sees $T/m$ entries instead of $T$.
\item \textbf{Top-$k$.} DSA scores the entries with a cheap indexer and keeps the
best $k$~\cite{deepseekv32}. Once the pool is bigger than $k$, every query
attends to $k$ entries and the layer becomes linear in $T$.
\item \textbf{Sliding window.} Some layers also keep the $w$ most recent tokens
uncompressed. Every query attends to all of them.
\end{itemize}

Pairs per layer are then
\[
  P(T) =
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{T^2}{2m} + wT, & T \le km, \\[2ex]
    kT - \dfrac{k^2 m}{2} + wT, & T > km.
  \end{cases}
\]
The two cases agree at $T = km$, where both give $k^2m/2 + wT$. Layers with
$m=1$, $k=\infty$, $w=0$ reduce to $T^2/2$.

\subsection{Model data}

Every constant below comes from the released config. Table~\ref{tab:app-arch}
lists them. KV layouts are GQA~\cite{ainslie2023gqa}, MLA (cache the compressed
latent plus the RoPE key, $r_{kv}=512$, $d_{\text{rope}}=64$)~\cite{deepseekv2},
and DeepSeek's CSA/HCA.

DeepSeek-V4-Pro has 61 layers. The first two are HCA, the rest alternate, giving
30 CSA and 31 HCA. CSA uses $m{=}4$ and $k{=}1024$, HCA uses $m'{=}128$ and no
top-$k$, and both keep a 128-token window~\cite{deepseekv4}. GLM-5 runs DSA on
all 78 layers with $k{=}2048$~\cite{glm5}. The other four use dense attention.

For the hybrid Qwen models, $L$ counts only the full-attention layers.
Qwen3-Next-80B has 48 layers with full attention every 4th ($L=12$). Qwen3.5-397B
has 60 layers in a 3:1 linear/full pattern ($L=15$). Their Gated DeltaNet layers
hold a fixed-size recurrent state, which we do not count as migratable KV.

\subsection{Effect of top-$k$ attention}

Top-$k$ changes the shape of the answer, not just the numbers. For a dense model
the replay term grows with $T^2$ while transfer grows with $T$, so the ratio
climbs with context and the decision flips at some context length. For a
top-$k$ model both terms are linear above $T=km$, so the ratio is flat and the
decision no longer depends on context length. This is why the GLM-5 panel is
close to horizontal past a few thousand tokens.

The same correction moves the two sparse models in Table~\ref{tab:app-crossover}.
Attention is the larger share of prefill for both. Charging it densely, as an
earlier version of this model did, put GLM-5 at $12.11$\,s and $5.93$\,Gbps and
DeepSeek-V4-Pro at $7.20$\,s and $1.10$\,Gbps.

\subsection{What we leave out}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{KV is BF16 for everyone.} DeepSeek-V4-Pro really stores FP8 with
BF16 only on the RoPE dimensions, about half of what we charge
it~\cite{deepseekv4}. We keep BF16 so all six are measured the same way. Cutting
its cache in half would halve its crossover. Quantization and allocator padding
are also ignored~\cite{kwon2023efficient}.
\item \textbf{Prefill is FLOPs over a fixed rate.} No kernel efficiency curve, no
batching, no tensor- or pipeline-parallel communication, no
queueing~\cite{agrawal2024sarathi}.
\item \textbf{The FFN term charges active parameters only.} The linear-attention
layers of the Qwen hybrids are folded into it rather than costed separately.
\item \textbf{No overlap, no reuse.} Transfer and prefill are each charged in
full and in series. We do not model pipelining KV transfer against compute, or
prefix cache hits at the destination~\cite{lmcache}. Both would favour transfer.
\item \textbf{Bandwidth is dedicated and flat.} No contention, no congestion
control, no RTT. Context bytes (4\,B/token of uint32 ids) are dropped from the
replay side. At $\eta \ge 9.7$\,KiB/tok they move the crossover by well under a
percent.
\item \textbf{One session.} This says nothing about many sessions sharing a link
and a prefill pool. That is the allocation problem in the main body.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Running it}

\begin{verbatim}
uv run python migration_ratio.py
uv run pytest test_migration_ratio.py
\end{verbatim}

\noindent
This writes \texttt{migration\_ratio.\{png,pdf\}} (ratio vs.\ bandwidth at
$T=100$k over $0.1$--$100$\,Gbps) and
\texttt{glm5\_context\_ratio\_bandwidths.\{png,pdf\}} (GLM-5 ratio vs.\ context
from $10^3$ to $10^7$ tokens, over 500 links spaced evenly in
$0.1$--$25$\,Gbps). There is no sampling and no seed.
