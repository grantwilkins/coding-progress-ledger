\section{Full Problem Formulation}

For compactness, define the source power value of session \(j\) as

\[
w_j
=
\delta_{i(j)j}\!\left(L_{i(j)}\right),
\]

and its steady serving demand at destination \(k\) as

\[
\phi_{jk}
=
\frac{f_j}{F_k(T_j)}
+
\frac{g_j}{G_k(T_j)}.
\]

The LP is then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\underset{{0\le x\le1}}{\text{maximize}}\quad
& \sum_{j,k} w_j y_{jk}
\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
& \sum_k y_{jk}\le1,
\\
& \sum_{\substack{j:i(j)=i\\k,a}}
  t_{ijk}^{a}x_{jk}^{a}
  \le S_iD,
\\
& \sum_{j,k}
  \left(
    C_jx_{jk}^{R}
    +K_{jk}x_{jk}^{K}
  \right)
  \le BD,
\\
& \sum_{j,a}
  t_{ijk}^{a}x_{jk}^{a}
  \le Q_kD,
\\
& b_k+\sum_j\phi_{jk}y_{jk}
  \le h_k,
\\
& M_k^{0}+\sum_jm_{jk}(H)y_{jk}
  \le M_k.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first constraint holds for each session \(j\). The second holds for
each source instance \(i\), and the final three instance-level
constraints hold for each destination \(k\). The network constraint is
shared across the source and destination site.

A physical plan requires \(x_{jk}^{a}\in\{0,1\}\). Allowing values in
\([0,1]\) gives the LP above and an upper bound on the source power that
can be moved under these limits.  The LP gives a clean statement of the problem, but Queue-Haul does not solve it in the control path. \section{Replay and KV Transfer}
\label{app:migration-ratio}

This appendix records the data and assumptions used in Figure~\ref{fig:single-session-crossover}. The plots are analytic as they combine published model configurations and hardware specifications, and contain no direct measurements.

\noindent\textbf{Computation.}
Consider a session with \(T\) tokens of context. Replay sends the token
sequence and runs prefill at the destination. KV transfer instead sends the
cache produced by that prefill. Their estimated times are

\[
t^{R}(T)
=
\frac{\Phi(T)}{\mathrm{EFF}},
\qquad
t^{KV}(T,\lambda)
=
\frac{8\eta(T)}{10^9\lambda},
\]

where \(\Phi(T)\) is the prefill work in FLOPs, \(\mathrm{EFF}\) is sustained
hardware throughput, \(\eta(T)\) is the KV size in bytes, and \(\lambda\) is
link bandwidth in Gbps. We plot $t^{R}/t^{KV}.$ Values above one favor KV transfer; values below one favor replay. The
crossover bandwidth is

\[
\lambda^\star
=
\frac{8\eta(T)}{10^9t^{R}(T)}.
\]

We estimate prefill work as
\[
\Phi(T)
=
\underbrace{2AT}_{\text{model weights}}
+
\underbrace{
2H_q(d_{qk}+d_v)
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} P_\ell(T)
}_{\text{attention}},
\]

where \(A\) is the active parameter count, \(H_q\) is the number of query
heads, \(d_{qk}\) and \(d_v\) are the query/key and value head dimensions, and
\(P_\ell(T)\) counts the causal query--key pairs processed by layer \(\ell\).

\noindent\textbf{Hardware.}
One \(8\times\)H100 SXM instance has 640~GB of HBM. Each GPU provides
\(989.5\)~TFLOP/s of dense BF16 tensor throughput. This is half of NVIDIA's
quoted \(1{,}979\)~TFLOP/s rate~\cite{nvidia2024h100}. Assuming a conservative
35\% model FLOP utilization~\cite{rpa} gives

\[
\mathrm{EFF}
=
8 \times 989.5\,\text{TFLOP/s} \times 0.35
\approx
2.77\,\text{PFLOP/s}.
\]

For each model, we use the minimum number \(N\) of instances that can hold its
FP8 weights plus 100~GB of runtime headroom, and set
\(\mathrm{EFF}=N\times2.77\)~PFLOP/s. This gives \(N=3\) for DeepSeek-V4-Pro,
\(N=2\) for Kimi-K2.6 and GLM-5, and \(N=1\) for the remaining models.
MFU is the only fitted hardware parameter. Replay time scales inversely with
MFU, while the crossover bandwidth scales linearly with it.

\noindent\textbf{Attention structure.}
Dense causal attention processes about \(T^2/2\) query--key pairs per layer.
Compression, top-\(k\) attention, and sliding windows alter this count. If one
KV entry represents \(m\) tokens, the layer attends over \(T/m\) entries.
Top-\(k\) attention caps this set at \(k\), while a sliding window retains the
most recent \(w\) tokens. The resulting pair count is

\[
P_\ell(T)
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{T^2}{2m}+wT,
& T\leq km,
\\[2ex]
kT-\dfrac{k^2m}{2}+wT,
& T>km.
\end{cases}
\]

The two cases agree at \(T=km\). Setting \(m=1\), \(k=\infty\), and \(w=0\)
recovers dense causal attention.

\noindent\textbf{Effect of top-\(k\) attention.}
Attention structure changes the shape of the crossover. For a dense model,
replay work grows as \(T^2\), while KV transfer grows as \(T\). Replay
therefore becomes less attractive as context grows. If every layer is capped
at \(k\), both costs become linear once \(T>km\), and the crossover stops
moving with context length. GLM-5 is close to this case because all 78 layers
use DSA.

DeepSeek-V4-Pro is mixed. Its 30 CSA layers are capped, but its 31 HCA layers
remain dense over a cache compressed by \(m=128\). A quadratic term therefore
remains, although with a small coefficient, and becomes visible only at very
long contexts. This makes DeepSeek-V4-Pro useful for the context-length sweep.
