\section{Replay/Transfer Ratio: Data, Model, and Assumptions}
\label{app:migration-ratio}

This appendix documents \texttt{migration\_ratio.py}, which produces
Figure~\ref{fig:migration-ratio} (ratio vs.\ inter-site bandwidth at fixed
context) and the GLM-5 context--bandwidth surface. Everything below is an
analytic roofline; no numbers here are measured.

\subsection{Cost model}

For a session of $T$ tokens we compare rebuilding serving state by
\emph{replay} (prefill from the prompt) against \emph{state transfer}
(shipping the KV cache). The reported quantity is the ratio
\[
  R(T,\lambda) \;=\; \frac{t_{\text{replay}}(T)}{t_{\text{transfer}}(T,\lambda)}
  \;=\; \frac{\Phi(T)/\mathrm{EFF}}{8\,\eta(T)/\lambda},
\]
with $R>1$ favouring KV transfer and $R<1$ favouring replay. Prefill work is
split into a dense feed-forward term linear in $T$ and a causal-attention term
quadratic in $T$:
\[
  \Phi(T) \;=\; \underbrace{2 A T}_{\text{FFN, active params}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\sigma\, L\, H_q\,(d_{qk}+d_v)\, T^2}_{\text{causal attention}},
\]
where $A$ is the activated parameter count, $L$ the number of layers holding
per-token KV, $H_q$ the query-head count, and $\sigma$ an architecture-specific
\emph{attention scale} capturing sequence-dimension compression (\S\ref{app:models}).
The $T^2$ (rather than $2T^2$) coefficient absorbs the causal factor of
$\tfrac{1}{2}$ against the $2$ FLOPs per multiply--accumulate. Transfer time uses
$\eta(T)$, the total KV bytes at $T$ tokens, converted to bits over a link of
$\lambda$ Gbps. Setting $R=1$ gives the crossover bandwidth
$\lambda^\star = 8\,\eta(T)/t_{\text{replay}}(T)$.

\subsection{Hardware}

One serving instance is $8\times$ H100 SXM. We take the dense BF16 tensor-core
peak as $989.5$\,TFLOP/s per GPU, i.e.\ half the $1{,}979$\,TFLOP/s figure NVIDIA
quotes with 2:4 structured sparsity~\cite{nvidia2024h100}. At a model FLOP
utilization~\cite{chowdhery2023palm} of $\mathrm{MFU}=0.35$ this gives a sustained
prefill budget
\[
  \mathrm{EFF} = 8 \times 989.5\,\text{TFLOP/s} \times 0.35 \approx 2.77\,\text{PFLOP/s}.
\]
MFU is the single free parameter in the model; $R$ scales linearly in it.

\subsection{Model data}
\label{app:models}

Architecture constants are read from each model's released configuration, not
fitted. Three KV layouts appear:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{GQA}~\cite{ainslie2023gqa}: $\eta = 2_{(K,V)}\cdot L \cdot H_{kv}
\cdot d_{\text{head}} \cdot 2\,\text{B}$, with $\sigma=1$.
\item \textbf{MLA}~\cite{deepseekv2}: only the compressed latent plus the RoPE
key are cached, $\eta = L\,(r_{kv} + d_{\text{rope}})\cdot 2\,\text{B}$ with
$r_{kv}=512$, $d_{\text{rope}}=64$; $\sigma=1$.
\item \textbf{CSA/HCA}~\cite{deepseekv4}: DeepSeek-V4-Pro compresses $m$ tokens
into one KV entry ($m=4$ for its 30 CSA layers, $m'=128$ for its 31 HCA layers),
caches a $c=512$ entry per compressed block plus a $c_I=128$ indexer key on CSA
layers, and keeps an $n_{\text{win}}=128$ uncompressed sliding window per layer.
Sequence-dimension compression carries into attention as
$\sigma = (30/4 + 31/128)/61 \approx 0.127$.
\end{itemize}

For the hybrid-linear Qwen models, $L$ counts only the full-attention layers:
Qwen3-Next-80B has $48$ layers at a full-attention interval of $4$ ($L=12$), and
Qwen3.5-397B has $60$ layers in a 3:1 linear/full pattern ($L=15$). Their
Gated~DeltaNet layers hold a constant-size recurrent state that we do not count
as migratable KV.

\subsection{Assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Uniform BF16 KV.} $\eta$ assumes two bytes per cached element for
every model. KV quantization, allocator padding, and block fragmentation are not
modeled~\cite{kwon2023efficient}. This is the assumption that makes the six
models comparable, but it is not what DeepSeek-V4-Pro actually stores: it uses
BF16 only for the RoPE dimensions and FP8 elsewhere, roughly halving its cache to
$\approx0.52$\,GB at $100$k~\cite{deepseekv4}, which would halve its crossover.
\item \textbf{Roofline prefill.} $t_{\text{replay}}$ is FLOPs over sustained
throughput at a fixed MFU, independent of $T$. It contains no kernel-efficiency
curve, no batching effects, no pipeline or tensor-parallel communication, and no
scheduler queueing~\cite{agrawal2024sarathi}.
\item \textbf{Dense FFN accounting.} The $2AT$ term charges activated parameters
only, and folds the linear-attention layers of the hybrid Qwen models into that
term rather than costing them separately.
\item \textbf{No overlap and no partial reuse.} Transfer and prefill are each
charged in full and serially; we model neither layer-wise pipelining of KV
transfer against compute nor prefix-cache hits at the
destination~\cite{lmcache}. Both would favour transfer.
\item \textbf{Link bandwidth is dedicated and flat.} $\lambda$ is a decimal-Gbps
goodput number with no contention, congestion response, or RTT term. Context
bytes ($\approx4$\,B/token of uint32 ids) are dropped from the replay side; at
$\eta \ge 9.7$\,KiB/tok they shift $\lambda^\star$ by well under a percent.
\item \textbf{Single session.} The ratio is per-session. It says nothing about
what happens when many sessions contend for the same link and prefill pool ---
that is the allocation problem the main body addresses.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Known gap: top-$k$ sparse attention}

The attention scale $\sigma$ models sequence-dimension \emph{compression} but not
the top-$k$ \emph{sparsity} that both DSA-family models apply on top of it, so
$\Phi(T)$ stays quadratic where the real models are close to linear. GLM-5 selects
\texttt{index\_topk}${=}2048$ entries per query~\cite{glm5}, and DeepSeek-V4-Pro
selects $1024$ compressed entries on its CSA layers~\cite{deepseekv4,deepseekv32}.
Charging attention as $\approx 2Tk$ pairs instead of $T^2/2$ at $T=100$k gives:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
& $t_{\text{replay}}$ (s) & $\lambda^\star$ (Gbps) \\
\midrule
GLM-5, as modeled        & $12.11$ & $5.93$ \\
GLM-5, with top-$k$      & $3.27$  & $22.0$ \\
DeepSeek-V4-Pro, as modeled   & $7.20$ & $1.10$ \\
DeepSeek-V4-Pro, with top-$k$ & $3.98$ & $2.00$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Attention is $76\%$ of GLM-5's modeled prefill cost and $51\%$ of
DeepSeek-V4-Pro's, so the correction is large for these two and nil for the four
dense-attention models. The direction is uniform: cheaper replay pushes
$\lambda^\star$ up, i.e.\ the figure \emph{understates} how far the replay region
extends for sparse-attention models. Table~\ref{tab:app-crossover} and the
figures report the uncorrected values.

\subsection{Reproduction}

\begin{verbatim}
uv run python migration_ratio.py   # figures + summary table
uv run pytest test_migration_ratio.py
\end{verbatim}

\noindent
Outputs are \texttt{migration\_ratio.\{png,pdf\}} (ratio vs.\ bandwidth at
$T=100$k, $\lambda \in [0.1,100]$\,Gbps) and
\texttt{glm5\_context\_ratio\_bandwidths.\{png,pdf\}} (GLM-5 ratio vs.\ context
for $T \in [10^3,10^7]$ over 500 links linearly spaced in $[0.1,25]$\,Gbps). The
run is deterministic; there is no sampling and no seed.
