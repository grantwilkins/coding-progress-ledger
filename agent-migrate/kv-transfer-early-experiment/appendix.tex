\section{Breakeven Analysis}
\label{app:breakeven}
This appendix gives the numbers behind Figure~\ref{fig:single-session-crossover} and its two figures. Everything here is from first principles on published specs. Nothing is measured.

\noindent\textbf{What we compute.} A session holds $T$ tokens of context. To move it to another site we can send the prompt and prefill again (\emph{replay}), or send the KV cache
(\emph{transfer}). We plot the ratio of the two times,
\[
  \frac{t^{R}}{t^{KV}} \;=\; \frac{\Phi(T)/\mathrm{EFF}}{8\,\eta(T)/\lambda},
\]
where $\Phi(T)$ is prefill FLOPs, $\mathrm{EFF}$ is sustained throughput, $\eta(T)$ is KV bytes, and $\lambda$ is the link rate in Gbps. Above $1$, send the KV cache. Below $1$, replay. The crossover rate is $\lambda^\star = 8\,\eta(T)/t^{R}(T)$.

Prefill has two terms:
\[
  \Phi(T) \;=\; \underbrace{2 A T}_{\text{FFN}} \;+\;
  \underbrace{2 H_q (d_{qk}+d_v)\, P(T)}_{\text{attention}},
\]
with $A$ the active parameter count, $H_q$ the query heads, and $P(T)$ the
number of causal (query, key) pairs.

\noindent\textbf{Hardware.} One instance is $8\times$ H100 SXM, $640$\,GB of HBM. Dense BF16 peak is $989.5$\,TFLOP/s per GPU. That is half of the $1{,}979$\,TFLOP/s NVIDIA quotes, because their number assumes 2:4 sparsity~\cite{nvidia2024h100}. At 35\% model FLOP utilization~\cite{chowdhery2023palm}, one instance sustains
\[
  \mathrm{EFF}_1 = 8 \times 989.5\,\text{TFLOP/s} \times 0.35 \approx 2.77\,\text{PFLOP/s}.
\]
Half the catalogue does not fit on one instance: at FP8 the weights alone are $1.6$\,TB for DeepSeek-V4-Pro, $1.0$\,TB for Kimi-K2.6, and $744$\,GB for GLM-5. We give each model the smallest number of instances $N$ that holds its FP8 weights plus $100$\,GB of runtime headroom, and prefill sees all of them, so $\mathrm{EFF} = N \cdot \mathrm{EFF}_1$. This only moves $t^{R}$. It leaves $t^{KV}$ alone, because $\eta$ is a per-token property of the architecture and does not care how many instances the weights are spread over. MFU is the only free parameter and the ratio scales linearly in it.

\noindent\textbf{Counting attention pairs.} Plain causal attention computes $T^2/2$ pairs per layer. Three architecture features change that count.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Compression.} CSA and HCA squeeze $m$ tokens into one KV entry~\cite{deepseekv4}. A layer sees $T/m$ entries instead of $T$.
\item \textbf{Top-$k$.} DSA scores the entries with a cheap indexer and keeps the best $k$~\cite{deepseekv32}. Once the pool is bigger than $k$, every query attends to $k$ entries and the layer becomes linear in $T$.
\item \textbf{Sliding window.} Some layers also keep the $w$ most recent tokens uncompressed. Every query attends to all of them.
\end{itemize}

Pairs per layer are then
\[
  P(T) =
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{T^2}{2m} + wT, & T \le km, \\[2ex]
    kT - \dfrac{k^2 m}{2} + wT, & T > km.
  \end{cases}
\]
The two cases agree at $T = km$, where both give $k^2m/2 + wT$. Layers with
$m=1$, $k=\infty$, $w=0$ reduce to $T^2/2$.

\noindent\textbf{Model data.} Every constant below comes from the released config. Table~\ref{tab:app-arch} lists them. KV layouts are GQA~\cite{ainslie2023gqa}, MLA (cache the compressed latent plus the RoPE key, $r_{kv}=512$, $d_{\text{rope}}=64$)~\cite{deepseekv2}, and DeepSeek's CSA/HCA.

DeepSeek-V4-Pro has 61 layers. The first two are HCA, the rest alternate, giving
30 CSA and 31 HCA. CSA uses $m{=}4$ and $k{=}1024$, HCA uses $m'{=}128$ and no
top-$k$, and both keep a 128-token window~\cite{deepseekv4}. GLM-5 runs DSA on
all 78 layers with $k{=}2048$~\cite{glm5}. The other four use dense attention.

For the hybrid Qwen models, $L$ counts only the full-attention layers.
Qwen3-Next-80B has 48 layers with full attention every 4th ($L=12$). Qwen3.5-397B
has 60 layers in a 3:1 linear/full pattern ($L=15$). Their Gated DeltaNet layers
hold a fixed-size recurrent state, which we do not count as migratable KV.

\noindent\textbf{Why DeepSeek-V4-Pro for the context panel.} Where every layer is capped at $k$, replay and transfer are both linear in $T$ above $T=km$, so the ratio is flat and the decision stops depending on context length. GLM-5 is that case: all 78 layers run DSA. DeepSeek-V4-Pro is the mixed one. Only its 30 CSA layers are capped, while its 31 HCA layers compress by $m'{=}128$ but attend densely, so a $T^2$ term survives with a small coefficient and the boundary still moves with context.

\noindent\textbf{What we leave out.} Weights are FP8 but KV is BF16 throughout, which is the conservative pairing and keeps the six comparable. DeepSeek-V4-Pro really stores FP8 with BF16 only on the RoPE dimensions, about half of what we charge it, and puts its routed experts in FP4, which would likely fit it in two instances rather than three~\cite{deepseekv4}. Multi-instance prefill is charged as $N$ times single-instance throughput, so expert-parallel all-to-all is free and the large models' crossovers are upper bounds. Prefill is FLOPs over a fixed rate, with no kernel efficiency curve, no batching, and no queueing~\cite{agrawal2024sarathi}; KV quantization and allocator padding are ignored~\cite{kwon2023efficient}. Transfer and prefill are each charged in full and in series, with no pipelining of transfer against compute and no prefix cache hits at the destination~\cite{lmcache}; both would favour transfer. Bandwidth is dedicated and flat. Context bytes (four-byte token ids) are dropped from the replay side, where at $\eta \ge 9.7$\,KiB/tok they move the crossover by well under a percent. The ratio is per-session and says nothing about many sessions sharing one link and prefill pool.
