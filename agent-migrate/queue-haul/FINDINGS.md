# Queue-Haul findings

The 2026-07-23 destination run proves migration correctness but does not
calibrate loaded admission. Its 4K/16K/24K service anchors delivered
91.4%–99.9% of the reused rates, and every replay/KV continuation matched.

The service frontier is not reducible:

- the agentic fit direction was already majority-infeasible at the minimum
  radius, so it has no feasible/infeasible bracket;
- the one-facet median predicted the tuning interactive-coding cell feasible
  when its lower probe was infeasible; and
- all 113 service summaries gave identical normal, emergency, and stable
  labels. Failures therefore dominated the three intended policy boundaries.

The loaded-migration rows also missed their treatment. Only 2 of 18 treatment
runs reached requested normalized load within ±0.05; achieved load ranged from
0 to 1.282. The run has no matched zero-load cell, and the reducer previously
substituted three old-runtime rows at slowdown 1. These measurements cannot
identify a load effect or support the checked-in profile.

As a diagnostic only, the new runtime took 54.5%–58.7% of the reused replay
prediction and 56.0%–65.6% of the reused 16K KV prediction. Separating this
runtime baseline from load interference predicts held-out 24K replay within
9.6%, but KV remains 22.5% low. Replay may support one baseline calibration and
one load multiplier; KV needs the targeted context/bandwidth interaction
before that claim is valid.

Queue-Haul now refuses censored service frontiers and destination-load misses.
Migration timing is reused base timing times a positive matched-runtime
calibration times a load-only slowdown of at least one. The checked profile
remains `estimated`; the immutable v7 bundle is diagnostic evidence, not a
destination profile.
